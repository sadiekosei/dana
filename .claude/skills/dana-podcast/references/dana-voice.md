# Dana's voice — write like her or don't bother

Source: Dana's blog on danaskaggs.com (read a few posts before writing;
they're short). Dana is a psychotherapist (18 years private practice),
podcaster ("Phoenix and Flame", top 5% globally), keynote speaker, and
self-described sassy Enneagram 8. Show notes, excerpts, and Libsyn
descriptions must sound like HER — not like a content agency.

## The sound of her

- **Sassy, wry, self-deprecating.** She jokes about being "vertically
  challenged," wonders if she's "outside his spit-range" watching Gordon
  Ramsay, imagines fleeing to Tahiti to sell seashell necklaces under
  witness protection. Humor lands mid-sentence, in asides.
- **Extended, concrete analogies carry the point.** The sinking canoe
  (overwhelm), the ladder in a warehouse (relationships), boomerangs vs
  sticks (avoided conversations), cramming steak (unsustainable pace).
  One vivid image per piece, worked hard.
- **Talks TO the reader, with questions.** "Is your canoe taking on
  water?" "What are YOU saying to YOURSELF?" "Do any of those
  experiences sound familiar?"
- **Personal stories with real specifics.** Her dad's "Papaw Days" and
  the Doritos ultimatum; the Zoom call where every third word sounds
  Russian; pacing the hallway snatching Starburst ("don't judge me").
- **Credibility worn casually,** dropped mid-story, never as a resume:
  "I've been a therapist in private practice for 18 years and started a
  podcast six years ago."
- **Punchy rhythm.** Short sentences. Fragments. Parentheticals (lots).
  Occasional ALL CAPS for emphasis. "TBH."
- **Em dashes: use sparingly** (Sadie, 2026-08-04). Prefer periods,
  commas, colons, or parentheses. When one truly earns its place, set
  it closed (word—word), never with spaces around it.

## Her signature vocabulary (use it — it's the brand)

- **"Boundaries sojourners"** — how she addresses her audience
- **"Your yard" / "not in your yard"** — her core boundary metaphor
  (episode 1 is literally "You're in my Yard!")
- **Mental Boardroom** — inner parts sitting at a table; you're the CEO
- **L.E.A.D. method** — Listen, Externalize, Assess, Direct
- **Curve balls, boomerangs, spinning plates, canoe/boxes**
- "Whoever holds the upset is the one who needs to speak."

## Rules for show notes in her voice

1. The episode summary should read like the opening of one of her blog
   posts: hook with a question or image, a dash of sass, then land the
   real problem the episode tackles. NOT "In this episode, X joins Dana
   to discuss..." — that's the agency voice she hired us to kill.
2. Bullets can keep structure but the phrasing gets her spin — benefit
   plus attitude, not corporate summary.
3. Never manufacture a personal story she didn't tell. Sass comes from
   phrasing and her established metaphors, not invented anecdotes. If
   she told a story IN the episode (it's in the transcript), that's
   fair game and usually the best hook.
4. SEO still applies (keywords in title, first sentence, excerpt) — the
   craft is making the keyword sentence sound like Dana said it.
5. Guest respect: the sass targets life, overwhelm, and Dana herself —
   never the guest.
