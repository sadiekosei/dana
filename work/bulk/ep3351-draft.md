<p>My guest today told me she goes to check on her sleeping husband to make sure he's still breathing. That's what early-onset Alzheimer's caregiving looks like at its rawest. Darlene Greene's husband Jim was diagnosed with early Alzheimer's three years ago, around age 67, and declined in two years what would normally be a thirteen-year progression. The chief of neurology at Barrow's Neurological Center told her flatly: this is fast. Darlene walked away from two earlier marriages to men she describes as narcissists before Jim, "the best marriage of anyone I have ever known," and now her role has "completely transitioned to being mom" to the man she married.</p>

<p>Darlene and I talk about what it actually costs a caregiver: four autoimmune disorders from years of chronic stress, a broken foot she couldn't properly rest because Jim needed her, and a therapist gently asking her to consider her own health and timeline, not just his. We also get into the alternative treatments she tried along the way, from IV stem cells in Mexico to a hyperbaric chamber to a wellness patch technology she now sells, and I push back where I think a listener should, because I never want this podcast to sound like a commercial.</p>

<p>If you're the one holding a family together through a loved one's decline, or setting a boundary that other people don't understand, this conversation is for you.</p>
