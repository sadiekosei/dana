<p>At 26, Falyn Morningstar was bloated, exhausted, losing her hair, and couldn't get a decent night's sleep no matter how many hours she logged. She went to her family physician. She went to her gynecologist. Both ran a basic blood panel, told her "you look good," and sent her home. Her labs were fine. She was not fine. That gap between "your numbers look normal" and "I feel awful" is where this whole conversation lives, and it's exactly the gap Falyn now spends her career closing as a certified Functional Diagnostic Nutrition (FDN) practitioner.</p>

<p>What made Falyn's story worth chasing down is where the trail led. Deeper testing (blood, saliva, stool) started connecting her physical symptoms to a pattern of emotional eating, and eventually to a childhood sexual trauma she had no conscious memory of until an image surfaced while reading a book years later. We talk about how a functional diagnostic nutrition test actually works, the three different reasons people emotionally eat, and somatic experiencing, the practice Falyn credits most for teaching her body it's safe now, decades after it decided it wasn't.</p>

<p>If you've ever been told your labs are fine while you feel anything but, or you've never quite been able to explain why food, sleep, or your own body feels like a battleground, this one's for you.</p>
