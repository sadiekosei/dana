<p>Some people quit a job. Danielle Sunberg quit big law in one sentence she didn't even plan to say. She'd just helped her team win a trial against a $6 billion judgment, and the partner on the phone told her he'd see her back in the office in two weeks. She thought about her actual office, that glass fishbowl in Washington, D.C., her stomach tightened, and "I quit" flew out of her mouth before she'd decided to say it. If you've ever felt that same tightening walking toward a job that looks perfect on paper, this conversation is for you.</p>

<p>Danielle is a wellness entrepreneur, transformational coach, and keynote speaker, and a former corporate litigator who spent years doing everything she'd been taught would guarantee lifelong happiness: work hard, get the prestigious job, win the case. She had all of it and still felt misaligned, so she went looking for why, and turned that search into her book "Atlas of Being." We talk about the persona she'd built as a "big law DC attorney," why the risk of staying eventually outweighed the risk of leaving, and what she learned traveling to Thailand and Greece with no one around who knew her as anything but herself.</p>

<p>We also get into her idea of "empty words," the agreements we sign onto without meaning to, and how learning to hear your own inner voice, what she calls the space underneath your thoughts, is the real first step toward realignment. If you've been quietly building a life that looks right from the outside but feels wrong from the inside, Danielle's story is a good place to start questioning it.</p>
