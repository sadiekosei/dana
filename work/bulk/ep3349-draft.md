<p><em>First aired July 28, 2024</em></p>

<p>Fifteen years into a career built entirely on asking hard questions, my guest today lost the ability to want to ask them. Julie Rose spent two decades as a national radio journalist, hosting a daily interview show on SiriusXM, and then a few years ago she realized she could no longer stomach engaging with the news, even though that was literally her job. She'd built an entire on-air identity around being fearless enough to ask what everyone else was thinking, and yet in her own personal life she wasn't having those conversations at all. Not even with her own mom, whose politics she couldn't figure out how to talk about without the conversation going nowhere good.</p>

<p>That crisis became Julie's whole second act: figuring out how to talk to people who disagree with you without either avoiding them or turning every exchange into a fight. Her answer, in one word, is curiosity, and she walks me through how she rebuilt that muscle, starting with reading the opinion piece she disagrees with most before she ever opens her mouth in real life. We get into the two hidden beliefs that wreck most hard conversations (that the point is to win, and that listening means agreeing), and a real-time example: a friend's provocative text about "cultural appropriation" that could have ended a 20-year friendship, and instead deepened it.</p>

<p>If you've ever gone quiet rather than risk a fight, or you're avoiding a conversation with someone you love because you're sure it'll go sideways, Julie's method is one of the most practical things you'll hear all year.</p>
