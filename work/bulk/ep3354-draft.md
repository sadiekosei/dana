<p><em>First aired October 1, 2024</em></p>

<p>Setting digital boundaries sounds simple until you actually try it: leave the phone in the car at the grocery store and see how long you last. My guest, marketing expert Emanuel Rose, does that on purpose. He also unplugs completely, no phone at all, for one full week every single month, so his brain has a chance to "detoxify" from what he calls the drop of dopamine every scroll delivers. That's not a productivity hack. It's how he stays a human being instead of what he calls a "thumb-clicking thing."</p>

<p>Emanuel has spent three decades in marketing and runs a digital agency, which makes his take on boundaries land differently: his literal job is to stay connected and one chapter ahead of every trend. But at 24 or 25 he went through a divorce, moved seven times, and lost his father, all inside about twelve months, and it pushed him into nature: fly fishing, backpacking, long stretches alone in "unmanicured space." That's where he learned the difference between using digital as a tool and living inside it as a lifestyle, and it's the same lesson he now brings to burned-out professionals drowning in their inboxes.</p>

<p>We talk about the practical version of all of this: how to schedule quiet time like you schedule everything else, how to use the accidental thirty seconds at a stoplight instead of reaching for your phone, and why the fastest way back to your own creativity is time spent completely disconnected.</p>
