<p><em>First aired August 11, 2024</em></p>

<p>My guest calls herself "terminally curious," and by the time you hear her story you'll understand why intentional optimism had to become her life's work. Andrea Johnson survived a 12-week inpatient program for bulimia and depression at 20, gastric bypass surgery not once but twice in three weeks (she snuck a shrimp when she wasn't supposed to), seventeen moves before she turned 21, and the loss of her mother to breast cancer. Her answer to all of it isn't sunny, toxic positivity. It's a taproot.</p>

<p>Andrea grew up on a mission compound in Seoul, South Korea, a third culture kid at an international school with 65 nationalities walking the same halls, which is where she learned a rule she still teaches executives today: the onus of communicating your message is on the communicator, not the listener. Now an executive and founder empowerment coach and a certified DISC consultant, she talks with me about the "taproot" she coaches clients to grow, meaning a rooted enough sense of who you are that criticism can't uproot you, and about the six tenets of intentional optimism she built out of watching her mother die well.</p>

<p>We also get into the question I couldn't NOT ask her: what do you do with a relationship to someone who's consistently dysfunctional and shows no sign of changing? Andrea's answer starts with knowing who you are before you ever decide whether to stay.</p>
