<p>My guest today told me her podcast intro calls are basically vetting sessions, because she doesn't want boring people on her show. "Small talk is virtually non-existent," she said. "It's basically like, hi, how are you doing? And one of my best friends said this: I don't care about the weather, I want to know what your deepest trauma is." That's Nikki LaCroce, and once we got into her deepest trauma, I understood exactly why she vets her guests. In early 2021, Nikki experienced what she calls her personal Armageddon: leaving a decade-long relationship with a covert narcissist while simultaneously losing her mother in the middle of the pandemic.</p>

<p>Nikki is a podcaster (Can I Just Say?) and, like me, someone who believes the most important conversations aren't the polished ones, they're the real ones. We talk about the difference between overt and covert narcissism (her ex was covert, which she says made the gaslighting and love bombing almost impossible to name at the time), the "pain threshold" that finally pushes someone to leave, and why one of her best friends telling her "you're asking for the bare minimum and you're not even getting that" was the sentence that changed everything.</p>

<p>We also get honest about the guilt that lingers after you leave, especially when leaving means you also lose time you'll never get back with people you love, and the deal-breakers exercise Nikki did to rebuild her own sense of self from the ground up.</p>
