<p>How do you make meditation actually useful when your whole career just went up in flames? Blair Abee found out the hard way. Twenty-five years into a spotless career, he blew the whistle on an abusive boss who was mismanaging funds and terrorizing her staff. There was an investigation. She got fired. And then, for his trouble, so did he: labeled a "troublemaker" for the crime of being right. His income disappeared in one afternoon. So did the identity he'd spent 25 years building. That's the pile-of-ash moment this show is named for, and it's exactly where his meditation practice stopped being a hobby and became survival.</p>

<p>I've sat across from enough high achievers in my therapy chair to know Blair's story isn't rare: the ego blow of losing the job that defined you, the terror of the blank space after. What makes Blair worth your time is what he did with that blank space. Forty years into a yoga and meditation practice, he went deeper than he ever had, started journaling for the first time in his life, and stumbled into what he calls "soul contact," a sense of a higher consciousness self standing right there with him. He built an entire meditation method, HIC (Higher Consciousness) Meditation, and five books out of that discovery.</p>

<p>We talk about the courage it took to speak up in the first place, why "peace, be still" became his go-to phrase, and what to actually do when the thought of sitting still terrifies you more than the chaos does.</p>
