<p><em>First aired March 9, 2025</em></p>

<p>Winnie Rodgers describes a specific, strange feeling: standing outside her own body, looking down at herself, thinking "nothing is wrong here" while everything in her actually knew something was wrong. Intellectually, on paper, her life checked every box. Her real estate business was thriving. But she'd built all her structure, her habits, her boundaries, for her work life, and none of it for her personal life. So when her personal life went sideways, she had nothing to catch her, and no practice noticing her own intuition telling her so.</p>

<p>Winnie is a top real estate agent in Charleston, South Carolina, and a marketing strategist with over two decades of experience who now teaches college classes on values and branding. We talk about what it actually looks like to trust your intuition instead of overriding it with your intellect, and the specific, real-world version of that problem for women in sales: the nights out, the drinks, the "cool girl" persona that keeps the deal alive, and how to say no to an inappropriate advance from someone with power over your business without burning the bridge. Winnie doesn't offer platitudes here. She tells you exactly what she says now, word for word, when someone crosses a line.</p>

<p>We also get into the morning ritual that keeps her grounded through a job that requires her to be "on" and shiny for everyone else, and why the industry's brutal first-year attrition rate has less to do with talent than with whether you've done the work to know yourself.</p>
