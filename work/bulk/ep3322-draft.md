<p>Adam Lucero went to a 2 p.m. movie by himself because he had four hours to kill in a city where he knew no one. He never saw it coming: a hard hit to the throat that he first mistook for a punch, then his arm, then his chest. By the time the lights came up, he was bleeding out on the theater floor, yelling at bystanders to call 911 while paramedics quizzed him on the president's name to keep him conscious. He woke up seven days later from a coma, six feet tall and 130 pounds, unable to walk, unable to eat without falling asleep at his plate, with the cognitive function of a four-year-old.</p>

<p>What Adam does with that story is the reason I wanted him on Phoenix and Flame. He had every reason, as I told him, to wrap himself in the victim cloak and wear it for the rest of his life. Instead, within a year of the attack, he ran a 4:59 mile. He talks about the moment he realized victimhood "wasn't benefiting my life in any way," the corporate job that felt like his soul being sucked out even after he'd fought so hard to get his body back, and the mindset work, energy science, and daily habits he now teaches as what he calls Superhuman performance.</p>

<p>We get into why spending time in total silence, no phone, no noise, is one of the most uncomfortable and most powerful things you can do for your mental health, how to actually kill procrastination instead of just feeling bad about it, and what it means to operate from commitment instead of emotion when you're trying to rebuild after trauma.</p>
