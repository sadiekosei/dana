<p><em>First aired September 5, 2023</em></p>

<p>Who labels a first-grader "the worst child ever"? A school diagnostician did, after testing Lois Letchford's son Nicholas and finding he could read 10 words, had "no strengths," and a low IQ. He wet his pants at school. He bit his fingernails. He stared into space while his teacher shouted at him. And that label, Lois told me, is exactly the kind of thing that follows a kid for life, because once "can't learn" gets in your head, that's all you can do. So Lois, a physical education teacher with zero training in reading instruction, decided to teach her own son herself.</p>

<p>What she found is the heart of this episode: her first real attempt failed too, until a friend told her to put away what wasn't working and make learning fun. So she started writing poetry, one tiny illustrated rhyme at a time, "Mug on a Bug," "Windmill on a Hill," and Nicholas started smiling instead of shutting down. That poetry-first method became the foundation of Lois's literacy work; she went on to co-found a program teaching students with dyslexia to read and write, and Nicholas went on to earn a PhD in Applied Mathematics from Oxford. We talk about the word that broke his teacher's whole approach ("saw" has three meanings, and Nicholas only knew one), why "he's dumb" is almost never the real diagnosis, and what Lois wishes she'd told every terrified, shut-down reader sooner: you are a future rocket scientist.</p>
