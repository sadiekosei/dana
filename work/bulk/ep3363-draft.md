<p>Picture a nine-year-old boy in rural Uruguay, walking up to a 21-year-old missionary and asking for bread. Jackson Calame didn't have any on him. It was 9:30 at night, they were rushing home, and he had to send the boy away hungry. The kid ducked his head and walked around him without a word. Jackson felt filthy, he says, comparing his own clean shirt and nice shoes to everything that boy didn't have. That night he decided he'd come back someday and open a little computer rental shop to help the town build an economy. Instead, he says, he felt his face turn to the right and heard a voice tell him to go home, use his education, and help towns like that one for the rest of his life. That's the moment that built the entrepreneur, and the boundaries lesson, at the center of this episode.</p>

<p>Jackson is a visionary-entrepreneur coach: founder of First Class Business, founder of the VisionPros Live podcast, and a 20% stakeholder in e-rational marketing, where he built out the client fulfillment process while the agency became one of Canada's 50 fastest-growing startups. He's also, by his own telling, twice divorced, a single father of four, and someone who has spent real time on the floor in a fetal position when entrepreneurship got to be too much. We talk about why setting boundaries as an entrepreneur isn't optional if you want to protect the vision you're building, why treating people (including the virtual assistants so many businesses treat as disposable) with dignity is both a moral stand and good business, and what to do with the doubting voices, yours and everyone else's, that show up the second you try to build something that matters.</p>

<p>By the end, Jackson makes the case that a midlife identity crisis or two isn't proof you took a wrong turn. Sometimes it's just the burning that comes before the rebuild.</p>
