<p><em>First aired December 2, 2024</em></p>

<p>My guest Carolyn Swora was in the middle of an ordinary work conversation with a kind colleague, being asked to do something completely unremarkable, when she suddenly burst into tears for no reason she could name. A friend called her afterward and asked if she was okay, then said something about being "trauma informed." Carolyn's response: "What is that? What's that word?" That question is where trauma-informed leadership starts, and it's the whole subject of this episode.</p>

<p>Carolyn is a leadership consultant, team coach, certified Dare to Lead facilitator, and two-time bestselling author. Her award-winning book, Evolve: The Path to Trauma-Informed Leadership, grew out of that unexplainable crying moment and 25 years of corporate experience, including 17 years in Big Pharma leadership, six years spent caring for her two young sons and her terminally ill husband, and an official burnout in 2007. We talk about what "trauma" actually means (hint: it doesn't require a machete), how her nervous system quietly ran her entire career without her knowing it, and why she now believes the body and brain were never separate to begin with.</p>

<p>You'll walk away with the actual symptoms of a nervous system on overload, the difference between big-T and little-t trauma, and why Carolyn now treats a sore back or a crawling scalp as her body talking to her instead of something to just push through.</p>
