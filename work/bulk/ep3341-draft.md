<p><em>First aired April 7, 2024</em></p>

<p>Remember "stop, drop, and roll," the thing they drilled into us in elementary school for if your clothes caught fire? My guest today has the equivalent for parenting anxiety, and honestly, we all need it, because if you're a parent right now you are being bombarded by shoulds. You should breastfeed at all costs. You should never breastfeed past a certain age. You should get your kid into the "right" preschool or you've ruined their life before they can talk. Dr. Alison Escalante calls this the ShouldStorm, and she should know: she was getting emails on her brand-new iPhone telling her that her newborn would have a lower IQ if she didn't breastfeed, "even though he had a severe medical condition that led him to have colitis and eight different protein allergies and bleeding." She kept trying anyway, until she started fainting.</p>

<p>Dr. Escalante is a pediatrician and the author of "Sigh, See, Start: How to Be the Parent Your Child Needs in a World That Won't Stop Pushing." She's also a TEDx speaker on a mission to ease the epidemic of anxiety that's convinced parents they're always failing. Her three-step method is built like stop, drop, and roll for your nervous system: sigh (a real, physiological reset for your fight-or-flight response), see (a few seconds of mindful noticing, not an hour of meditation you don't have time for), and start (running your own small experiment instead of waiting to get it perfect).</p>

<p>We get into how social media supercharges the ShouldStorm without creating it, why "sometimes you start nothing" is legitimate parenting advice, and why Dr. Escalante says the goal was never perfection, it's confidence in your own ability to figure it out.</p>
