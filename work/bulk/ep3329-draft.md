<p>Picture a fifteen-year-old boy cranking the shower scalding hot right before he gets out, on purpose, so the mirror fogs over and he never has to see himself. That was Dai Manuel's daily trick for years, until the one morning he got rushed and looked anyway. He didn't recognize the kid staring back. That's the moment my guest points to as where change is possible actually started for him: not five years of weight gain undone in a flash, but an ugly, cathartic cry in front of a bathroom mirror that turned into twenty months of small, boring, unglamorous bites forward.</p>

<p>You might know Dai as "Coach Moose" in CrossFit circles, a nickname earned during a brutal intro workout that left him dry-heaving against a chain-link fence. He went on to become an award-winning speaker, author, and former partner and COO of a multimillion-dollar retail company. But the resume isn't why he's on Phoenix and Flame. It's because underneath it he spent fifteen years, in his own words, "managing this emotional roller coaster with alcohol," propping up a persona he calls "fun guy Dai," until his wife asked him one question that ended it: "Are you being the type of man that you would want to marry your daughters?" We get into what actually gets someone through the work of real change (hint: it's rarely dramatic), and the Five F's, Fitness, Faith, Family, Finances, with an overarching roof of Fun, that he now uses as a filter for every yes and no in his life.</p>

<p>If you've ever wanted permission to say no without an essay of excuses attached, Dai's got a phrase for you: "No is a complete sentence." Stick around for how a boy who avoided cameras for years built a life, and a framework, sturdy enough to weather anything.</p>

<h2>About Dai Manuel</h2>
<p>Dai Manuel, known in the CrossFit world as "Coach Moose," is an award-winning digital thought leader, author, distinguished Toastmaster, and keynote speaker. He's a former partner and chief operating officer of a multimillion-dollar retail company, and the author of The Whole Life Fitness Manifesto, built on the Five F's framework he developed after what he describes as "15 years of me basically managing this emotional roller coaster with alcohol." He's based in Vancouver, Canada.</p>
