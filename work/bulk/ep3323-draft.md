<p>Susan Gold remembers coming home from school one day and seeing her father's clothing flying out the second-floor window onto the front lawn. She raced to pick up each piece as fast as her mother could throw it down, trying to keep her father in the house, because she didn't think she'd survive without what she calls his "pseudo protection." That's the kind of chaos Susan grew up in as the middle child of five, and it's exactly the kind of toxic family story I wanted on Phoenix and Flame, because so many of my listeners are living some version of it right now.</p>

<p>Professionally, Susan Gold is the kind of name-dropper you actually believe: she attached celebrity talent to projects in New York and Los Angeles, talked Andy Warhol into an on-camera Pontiac commercial, helped launch Fox News Channel at Roger Ailes' request, and got Ben Stiller, Jack Black, Taylor Swift, and David Beckham interviewed by cartoon characters for Disney Channel. But the reason she's on this show is the book she wrote about what came before all of that, "Toxic Family: Transforming Childhood Trauma into Adult Freedom," and the years of addiction, clinical depression, and narcissistic relationships that came after it.</p>

<p>We talk about the difference between forgiving your parents for what happened to them and still honoring what happened to you, why the "point one finger, three point back at you" axiom can trap empaths in abusive relationships, and how Susan finally learned to stop carrying weight that was never hers to carry. If you've ever felt like you had to be perfect to be safe, this conversation is for you.</p>
