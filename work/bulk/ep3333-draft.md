<p>If somebody looked up the definition of a people-pleaser, they would have found a picture of Jennifer Schwytzer. That's how she describes her old self, seventeen years deep into a career as a licensed social worker, giving and giving until, in her words, "you have nothing left for you." The wake-up call was simple and brutal: if you're not first, you're last.</p>

<p>Jennifer is a certified life coach and founder of Kindfulness Coaching, where she helps professional women trade chronic stress and overwhelm for boundaries that actually stick. We talk about why women in particular get conditioned to hold back, the friends she lost the moment she started saying no, and how she figured out who in her life was a cheerleader for her growth versus who was just watching from the sidelines.</p>

<p>We also get into what burnout actually feels like day to day (hint: it's quieter and sneakier than you'd think), how to tell it apart from depression, and why "priorities," not "goals," is the word she starts every client with.</p>
