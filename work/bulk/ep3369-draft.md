<p>Picture an 11-year-old kid clocking hours at a hair salon, doing everything except the actual hair, because nobody else was going to hand her a better life. By 14 she was assistant manager of a shoe store, bossing around employees twice her age. By 24 she was a chief operating officer. That's how Victoria Pelletier's leadership journey started, and it's the answer to a question a lot of us are quietly asking: how does someone build real, lasting success out of a childhood that gave her nothing to build on?</p>

<p>Victoria is a 20-plus-year corporate executive, board director, bestselling author, and the exec her former colleagues nicknamed the "Turnaround Queen" and "the CEO Whisperer." For years she led like the other nickname she earned: the Iron Maiden. Encased, closed off, no vulnerability allowed. It took one gut-punch comment from a colleague to make her rebuild into what she now calls a turtle instead (tough shell, soft head poking out). We get into that shift, why she's an outspoken advocate for DEI as an openly queer woman who was "the only one" in the room for years, and the actual boundaries, the calendar blocks, the delegated laundry, the one rule she breaks on purpose, that let her run a life instead of just surviving one.</p>

<p>You'll walk away with real language for setting boundaries at work without apologizing for them, and a clear case for why inclusive workplaces perform better, not just feel nicer.</p>
