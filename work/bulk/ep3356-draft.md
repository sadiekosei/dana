<p>Twenty-seven surgeries. That's the number my guest Shelly Starks counted by the time she reached a radical hysterectomy that forced a four-month wait before she could even start hormone replacement therapy. She calls that four months "the most raw that I could possibly be," and that rawness is exactly where setting boundaries at work has to start: not with a script, but with finally admitting what isn't working.</p>

<p>Shelly is the CEO of Inline Consulting Services, an extended DISC coach and business advisor with more than 20 years of experience helping teams and individuals turn behavioral styles into scalable growth. She built that expertise the hard way: a 20-year marriage that ended after years of caregiving for a daughter born needing surgery just to eat, breast cancer at 26, and a body that, in her words, kept "growing things... very large and very fast" until surgery number 27. We talk about how she turned all of that into eight separate revenue streams, and why workplace boundaries start with figuring out whether the person across from you is task-oriented or people-oriented before you ever open your mouth.</p>

<p>You'll walk away with Shelly's own boundary script (ask permission before you disagree, every time), her DISC-based fix for a boss who skips "good morning" and jumps straight to the task list, and my own three-word method for finally processing what you've been shoving down: face it, feel it, free it.</p>
