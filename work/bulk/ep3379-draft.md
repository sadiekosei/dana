<p>A patient once told me, in confidence, that his love language was physical touch, but he couldn't just ask for it. So instead he leaned into a reputation as a player and used sex as the vehicle to get the one thing he actually wanted: to be held. That story is exactly why traditional masculinity is failing so many modern relationships, according to my guest today, licensed marriage and family therapist Kristal Desantis. Men are more touch-starved than most of us realize, she says, and the old script never gave them another way to ask for it.</p>

<p>Kristal specializes in relationships, trauma work, and sex therapy, and her insights have been featured in Newsweek, HuffPost, The Good Men Project, and Time. She grew up surrounded entirely by women, in a family of six girls, then moved from Japan to an American women's college and got her first real look at male culture working in a sports bar. That outsider curiosity became her life's work: understanding why the old three P's of masculinity (protector, provider, procreator) collapsed almost overnight, while nobody built men a new script to replace it.</p>

<p>We get into the "masculinity trap" (criticized for being too stoic, then judged the moment you open up), why so many men are touch-starved, and the first two pillars of Kristal's STRONG model: safety and trust. If you've ever wondered why the men in your life seem damned if they do and damned if they don't, this one's for you.</p>
